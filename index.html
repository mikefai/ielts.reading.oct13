Before you begin

Build a small, reliable toolkit.

You do not need to understand every line before you answer. Give each reading move a clear job.

01

Skim for shape

Read the title, opening, topic sentences, and conclusion. Notice how the argument or story is organised.

GET THE MAP
02

Scan for an anchor

Search for names, dates, numbers, or a distinctive idea from the question. Then slow down at the likely location.

FIND THE PLACE
03

Track the meaning

Expect paraphrase. Match the idea in the question to the passage, even when the vocabulary changes.

FOLLOW THE IDEA
04

Budget with flexibility

Share your hour across the passages in a way that suits their length and difficulty. If you are stuck, mark the item, move on, and return.

THE FOUR QUESTION TYPES

Each method follows the same rhythm: understand the task, locate the proof, check the answer.

4 modules →
01JUDGE THE CLAIM

True / False / Not Given

Separate proof from assumption.

EVIDENCE FIRST
✳

Golden rule Decide only from what the passage says: support is True, contradiction is False, and missing information is Not Given.

THE METHOD Three deliberate checks

  1. 1

    Break down the claim. Notice its subject, action, and any limiting words such as all, never, or a date.

  2. 2

    Find the matching lines. Scan for an anchor, then read around it for paraphrase or contrast.

  3. 3

    Classify the relationship. Confirm, contradict, or leave it unproven. Do not fill gaps with common sense.

↳

Your move Underline the claim's strongest word before you search. It often changes the answer.

PASSAGE EXTRACTFIELD STUDY / 06
“Researchers compared the roof surface temperature of six planted buildings with six nearby bare roofs. On sunny afternoons, the planted roofs averaged 2.4°C cooler. After sunset, the difference was small.”
HOW THE EVIDENCE CHANGES THE ANSWER
STATEMENTTEXT EVIDENCECONCLUSION
Planted roofs were cooler on sunny afternoons.They averaged 2.4°C cooler.TRUE — supported.
The difference was large after sunset.The difference was small.FALSE — contradicted.
Residents felt more comfortable.No resident opinions are reported.NOT GIVEN — unaddressed.

The passage measures roof temperature. It gives no report of residents’ opinions.

QUICK CHECK Researchers asked residents whether they preferred planted roofs. The passage reports the survey results.

02MATCH THE MAIN IDEA

Matching Headings

Name what the paragraph does.

WHOLE PARAGRAPH
✳

Golden rule Choose the heading that captures the paragraph’s main idea, not the heading that merely sounds broad or repeats one detail.

THE METHOD Read for the paragraph’s job

  1. 1

    Read the paragraph first. Notice the opening, the shift words, and the final point.

  2. 2

    Sum it up in a few words. Make your own short label before looking at the heading list.

  3. 3

    Test the best fit. Check that the heading covers the whole paragraph. Cross out a detail-only match.

↳

Your move Write a three-to-five-word summary in the margin before you select.

PARAGRAPH CRESTORING THE CANAL
“The canal’s first clean-up improved water quality, but the city’s larger aim was to reconnect neighbourhoods to the waterfront. It added a continuous walking path, opened old towpaths, and converted two warehouses into public workshops. The canal is now part of daily city life rather than a hidden service route.”
POSSIBLE HEADINGS
  • A How water samples are collected
  • B A waterway restored as a public space
  • C The decline of canal transport
QUICK CHECK Which heading best fits the paragraph above?

03COMPLETE WITH CARE

Sentence & Summary Completion

Let the instruction set the limit.

COPY WITH CARE
✳

Golden rule Follow the task’s word limit exactly; when it says to use words from the passage, copy the answer wording exactly.

THE METHOD Make the answer fit

  1. 1

    Read the instruction. Note the maximum number of words and whether to use passage words.

  2. 2

    Predict the gap. Use grammar and meaning to anticipate the kind of answer needed.

  3. 3

    Locate and copy. Find the matching idea, copy the exact words requested, and check spelling and fit.

↳

Your move Count each answer word, then read the completed sentence aloud in your head.

PASSAGE EXTRACTHABITAT SURVEY
“During winter, the team surveyed freshwater marshes. In spring, they moved to coastal grasslands.”
COMPLETE THE SENTENCE

During winter, the team surveyed freshwater marshes.

NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS FROM THE PASSAGE

The two-word answer is copied from the winter sentence. The spring location is different.

QUICK CHECK In spring, the team moved to ______. Choose the exact passage words.

04CHOOSE THE PROVEN ANSWER

Multiple Choice

Answer the question in front of you.

PROOF OVER PLAUSIBILITY
✳

Golden rule Select the option that answers this specific question and agrees with passage evidence; grammar alone is never proof.

THE METHOD Verify the option

  1. 1

    Read the question carefully. Pin down what it asks before weighing the options.

  2. 2

    Locate the relevant evidence. Scan for an anchor, then read the complete sentence and its context.

  3. 3

    Eliminate and confirm. Reject choices that are contradicted, only partly true, or not mentioned. Match the remaining choice to the question.

↳

Your move Point to the sentence that proves your choice. If you cannot, keep checking.

PASSAGE EXTRACTCOOLING THE CLASSROOM
“To reduce afternoon glare without blocking airflow, designers fitted the south windows with narrow timber slats. The angle could be adjusted as the sun moved across the sky.”
QUESTION

Why were narrow timber slats fitted to the south windows?

↗ Look for the stated purpose.
QUICK CHECK Why were the slats fitted?
